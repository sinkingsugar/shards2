## 2026-10-06-cecbba1-codex-55d37a#F1
- Disposition: fixed
- Change/reason: `run_with_timeout` starts each command in its own process group (`process_group(0)`) and kills the whole group when the command exits or times out; readers report through a channel with a 2 s grace, and the stdin writer is detached, so no join can outlive the deadline. The first version shelled out to `kill -KILL -<pid>`; Ubuntu CI showed procps `kill` did not kill the group (stdout came back empty after the grace), while local util-linux `kill` did. 0a9b00e calls `kill(2)` through `libc` instead and tightens the test bounds below the grace, so a failed kill now fails the test rather than passing slowly.
- Evidence: `bench/authoring/src/main.rs` test `timeouts_and_exits_kill_descendants_holding_the_pipes` (unix): `sh -c "sleep 30 & wait"` with a 100 ms timeout returns timed out in under 1.5 s; `sh -c "echo hi; sleep 30 &"` returns status 0 with stdout `hi` in under 1.5 s (the shell exits first, the descendant holding stdout is killed). Passes locally and in CI at 0a9b00e1a6626ddabb837e746a230e5bdce39da2 (ubuntu and macos).
